Skull emitters · PixiJS v8 · @pylinka/core (GPU simulation) · Spine 4.2 · a port of particles_demo (Particlr)

Three setups, one emitter each, picked from the menu. Each is a Pylinka node graph built in code (src/skullEffects.ts) and simulated entirely on the GPU; the app sets the screen-relative positions through Pylinka knobs. The skulls are a flipbook baked from the Spine 'still' loop at startup: 40 frames of 96 px (~31 fps), plus a mirrored copy of each frame for skulls facing the other way. Only the shown setup is simulated. # particles sets how many skulls are alive at once; changing it rebuilds the emitter, because a Pylinka pool's size is fixed when it is built.
  • Emitter: Cascade — # particles alive at once (births run 50% ahead of deaths to keep the pool full) 56–80 px skulls fired upward in a 30° fan at 300–360 px/s, under 450 px/s² gravity; they land on a floor 80 px below the emitter and bounce (55% vertical speed kept, 80% sideways); 2.5–3.5 s life, growing from half size to full; fades in over the first 15% of life, holds at 90% opacity, then fades out in the last 179–250 ms. Each skull has a random tilt (±180°) and faces left or right at random. From 1.65 s of age (when a skull typically first lands) it spins at a random 90–360 °/s either way.
  • Emitter: Birds — # particles skulls flying left to right in a 300 px band, entering and leaving off screen (they die just past the right edge, and a new one enters on the left). Speed is random, 40–120 px/s, and size follows it (32–90 px), so smaller birds are slower. All face their flight; no tilt or fade. Simulated 40 s ahead at startup so the flock already spans the screen.
  • Emitter: Swirl — the cascade's fountain fired from lower down (85% of the way down the open band), its emitter swinging left and right on a 2 s sine wave (±25% of the width; y fixed), with no gravity or floor, pulled by two attractors at (25%, 50%) and (75%, 50%) of the band. Each pulls straight at its point at up to 3,000 px/s², easing smoothly to nothing at a radius of 1.2× its distance from the emitter. Mild drag (1) lets skulls spiral in. Moving the emitter leaves skulls already in flight where they are.
  • Differences from particles_demo — Pylinka keeps every particle on the GPU, so nothing that needs per-particle CPU access carries over: there is no Spine mode (a live skeleton per particle); a cascade skull gets one spin from about when it lands instead of a new one at every bounce; mirroring is a second row of the flipbook rather than a negative scale; skulls draw in pool order, not sorted oldest or fastest in front; and each skull's flipbook starts on frame 0 rather than a random frame.
Each Pixi tick: runtime.update(dt) queues a step, and Pylinka runs it on the GPU when Pixi renders the view.
